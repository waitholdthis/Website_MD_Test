# Sanctuary Digital — In-Depth Business Model

*Companion to the [12-Month Business Plan](church_web_agency_business_plan.md). This document defines the operating business model — how the company creates, delivers, and captures value — and is paired with a live financial model (Excel workbook) that turns every assumption below into a 12-month P&L, unit-economics, and breakeven analysis.*

---

## 1. Business Model Canvas

### 1.1 Customer Segments
- **Primary: small-to-midsize Protestant/non-denominational churches** (roughly 50–1,000 weekly attendance) in a defined local metro, run by a part-time or bi-vocational communications staffer, volunteer, or the pastor personally — the largest slice of the ~355,000–373,000 U.S. congregations and the segment most underserved by both DIY platforms and national agencies ([Hartford Institute](https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/fast-facts-on-american-religion/)).
- **Secondary: multi-campus churches and regional denominational networks** (e.g., district offices, church-planting networks) — lower volume, higher contract value, and a channel for bulk client acquisition.
- **Explicitly not targeting**: mega-churches with in-house web/marketing teams (too small a pool, too competitive against enterprise vendors), and churches wanting the cheapest possible DIY tool (that's Tithe.ly Sites' territory, not a margin-accretive fight to pick).

### 1.2 Value Propositions
- **For the pastor/decision-maker**: "A website that actually looks like your church, handled by people who show up" — removes the anxiety of picking a vendor and the embarrassment of an outdated site, without demanding a five-figure upfront check.
- **For the church administrator/volunteer**: One local point of contact for website, giving, sermons, and prayer requests — replacing a patchwork of logins and the burden of being "the tech person" on top of their real job.
- **Quantifiable value**: predictable $100–500/mo cost structure (vs. $2,500–10,000+ upfront for a custom agency build), ministry-specific functionality no generic web agency understands out of the box, and a maintenance relationship that compounds instead of ending at launch.

### 1.3 Channels
- **Outbound**: founder-led cold-calling to church offices (Section 7 of the business plan), in-person visits, local ministerial-alliance attendance.
- **Referral/partnership**: denominational and church-network distribution deals (mirroring [Ministry Designs' Nazarene partnership](https://nazarene.org/news/usa-canada-region-partners-with-ministry-designs-for-church-websites/)), client-to-client referrals with a service-credit incentive.
- **Inbound**: agency website/portfolio, local SEO, a "state of local church websites" authority report, light paid search once cash flow supports it (Phase 4+).
- **Retention channel**: direct relationship (phone, quarterly check-in calls) rather than a ticketing system — this is itself a channel differentiator, not just a support cost.

### 1.4 Customer Relationships
- **High-touch, named point of contact** for every account — not a rotating support queue. This is the core relationship promise that justifies the price premium over Tithe.ly/DIY tools.
- **Proactive, not reactive**: the maintenance relationship is built around scheduled quarterly "digital health checks" that surface upgrade opportunities before the client has to ask.
- **Community embeddedness**: local sponsorships, ministerial-alliance participation, and in-person kickoff/training sessions build a relationship that a remote national vendor structurally cannot replicate.

### 1.5 Revenue Streams
See Section 2 below for the full build — summarized: (a) tiered monthly subscription revenue (Foundation/Growth/Ministry Partner), (b) one-time setup fees for clients who don't take the 12-month waiver, (c) recurring add-on/expansion revenue (SEO, Google Ad Grant management, media production), (d) multi-campus/custom contracts as a longer-term stream.

### 1.6 Key Resources
- **Reusable design system and component library** — the asset that makes "custom design" economically deliverable at $100–500/mo instead of $5,000+ one-off pricing.
- **Founder's design/development capability** (Months 1–6) and a subcontracted designer/developer relationship (Month 7+) for build capacity.
- **Local relationship capital** — the ministerial-alliance and denominational-office relationships that are slow to build but hard for a competitor to copy.
- **Integration expertise** with the giving/ChMS platforms churches already run (Planning Center, Pushpay, Tithe.ly, Breeze).

### 1.7 Key Activities
- New client discovery, design, build, and launch (the onboarding SOP).
- Outbound sales (cold calling, networking, partnership development).
- Ongoing account management: content updates, quarterly health checks, upsell conversations.
- Add-on service delivery (SEO management, Google Ad Grant management, media/content production).

### 1.8 Key Partnerships
- **Giving/ChMS platforms** (Planning Center, Pushpay, Tithe.ly, Breeze) — not competitors but integration partners; the agency's value is making these tools work well inside a custom site, not replacing them.
- **Denominational offices and church-planting networks** — the highest-leverage distribution partnership available in this market.
- **Hosting/infrastructure vendor** (managed WordPress or equivalent) — a reseller-tier relationship keeps per-client hosting COGS low as the client base scales.
- **Subcontract designer/developer** — the mechanism for adding build capacity without full-time payroll risk in Year 1.

### 1.9 Cost Structure
See Section 3 below — cost-driven, not value-driven, at this stage: the model is optimized to keep COGS (hosting, payment processing, build labor) and OpEx (founder draw, contractor retainer, tools, marketing) as low as possible per client while investing in the design-system asset that lets one operator deliver custom work at scale.

## 2. Revenue Model Deep-Dive

Four revenue streams, each modeled explicitly in the accompanying workbook (`Revenue Build` tab):

| Stream | Mechanics | Year-1 role |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurring subscription (MRR)** | Foundation $100/mo, Growth $250/mo, Ministry Partner $500/mo, blended at a 60/30/10 target mix → **$185/mo blended ARPU** (Assumptions tab, blended row) | The core, compounding stream — every active client adds to a growing base that doesn't require re-selling each month |
| **Setup fees** | $1,200 / $1,800 / $2,500 by tier, **waived for clients who take a 12-month commitment** (modeled at an 80% waiver take-rate) | A small one-time offset to build cost for the ~20% of clients who prefer month-to-month flexibility; intentionally traded away for most clients in exchange for revenue predictability |
| **Add-on / expansion revenue** | Local SEO, Google Ad Grant management, media/content production — modeled at a $250/mo average per attached client, ramping from a 0% attach rate in Month 1 to a 30% attach rate by Month 12 | The highest-margin, fastest-growing stream — see Section 5 (maintenance/expansion strategy) |
| **Multi-campus/custom contracts** | ~$1,000+/mo per contract, not modeled in the Year-1 base case (assumed a Year 2+ stream) | Optionality that the model deliberately doesn't count on for Year 1 credibility |

**Modeled outcome (Year 1, base case):** 21 active clients and **$3,800 MRR** by Month 12, **$40,973 total Year-1 revenue**, per the `Revenue Build` and `P&L` tabs. This is a deliberately conservative, founder-led ramp — not a hockey-stick projection — because the sales motion (cold calling + local relationships) scales linearly with founder time until a channel partnership or a hire changes the slope.

## 3. Cost Structure

### 3.1 Cost of Goods Sold (variable, scales with clients)
| Cost | Driver | Modeled rate |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting | Per active client, blended across tiers | $18.80/mo blended (`Assumptions` tab) |
| Payment processing | % of revenue (Stripe-equivalent) | 3.0% of revenue |
| Build labor | Per new client, only counted as a **cash** cost once a paid subcontractor performs the build (Month 7+) | 20.5 hrs blended × $50/hr = **$1,025 per new client** |

**Modeling note (important):** in Months 1–6, the founder personally builds every site. That time is not double-counted as a cash COGS line — it's already represented by the Founder Draw in OpEx. Once a paid subcontractor takes over builds (Month 7+), build labor becomes a real cash cost. This distinction is what keeps the P&L honest instead of showing an artificial loss from counting the same hour of labor twice.

### 3.2 Operating Expenses (mostly fixed, step-changes at hiring/marketing milestones)
| Line | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Founder draw | Ramps $2,000/mo (Month 1) → $5,000/mo (Month 12), linear |
| Contractor retainer | $0 through Month 6, $1,500/mo from Month 7 onward |
| Software & tools | Flat $200/mo (CRM, design tools, project management, phone/dialer) |
| Marketing & lead gen | $200/mo (Months 1–6) → $400/mo (Months 7–12) |
| Insurance / legal / admin | Flat $150/mo |
| One-time startup costs | $1,500 in Month 1 only (LLC formation, initial tooling, business cards) |

### 3.3 Why this cost structure matters strategically
The design-system/component-library investment (a Key Resource, not a cost line here) is what lets build labor sit at ~20.5 hours blended instead of the 40–80+ hours a from-scratch custom build normally takes — that efficiency is the entire mechanism by which "custom design" can be sold at $100–500/mo instead of $2,500–10,000 upfront. Protecting and continuously reinvesting in that system is the single highest-leverage cost decision in the model.

## 4. Unit Economics

All figures below are computed live in the `Unit Economics` tab of the workbook and update automatically if you change any assumption.

| Tier | Monthly Price | Gross Margin ($/mo) | Gross Margin (%) | Avg. Customer Lifetime* | LTV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | $100 | $82 | 82.0% | 33.3 months | $2,733 |
| Growth | $250 | $220.50 | 88.2% | 33.3 months | $7,350 |
| Ministry Partner | $500 | $453 | 90.6% | 33.3 months | $15,100 |
| **Blended (weighted)** | **$185** | **$160.65** | **86.8%** | **33.3 months** | **$5,355** |

*Average lifetime = 1 ÷ mature-phase monthly churn (3.0%), i.e., a client is assumed to stay active roughly 33 months on average once past the early ramp-up churn window.

**Acquisition cost:**
- Blended S&M cost (mature months): $400/mo marketing spend + $2,598/mo imputed founder sales-time cost (15 hrs/week × 4.33 weeks × $40/hr opportunity cost) = **$2,998/mo**
- Divided by ~2.2 new clients/month (mature-phase average) → **Blended CAC ≈ $1,384 per new client**
- **CAC payback period ≈ 8.6 months** (blended CAC ÷ blended monthly gross margin)
- **LTV : CAC ratio ≈ 3.9x** — healthy by SaaS/services benchmarks (>3x is generally considered a sound ratio), though tighter than businesses with near-zero-cost sales motions, because this model honestly imputes the founder's own selling time as a real cost rather than pretending founder-led sales are free

**One-time build cost per client** (a separate, non-recurring cost tracked alongside CAC): Foundation $750, Growth $1,250, Ministry Partner $2,000, blended $1,025 — this is the true "cost to deliver" figure that should inform any future decision to discount setup fees or run promotions.

**What this tells you:** the business earns back its acquisition and build cost in under 9 months per client on average, and every month after that is high-margin (87% blended) recurring profit — which is why the maintenance/retention motion (Section 5) matters more to long-run profitability than the initial sale.

## 5. Scaling Maintenance & Expansion Revenue

The unit economics above make the strategic priority explicit: **a client's value is realized in months 9+ of the relationship, not at signing.** Three mechanisms drive this in the model and in practice:

1. **Attach-rate ramp on add-ons.** The model assumes the add-on attach rate grows linearly from 0% to 30% of the active base over Year 1 (`Revenue Build` row 26) — meaning by Month 12, roughly 6 of 21 clients are paying an average $250/mo on top of their base tier. This is the fastest-growing, highest-margin line in the model because it carries no incremental build-labor COGS.
2. **Tier upgrades within the base.** As churches add ministries (small groups, livestream, multi-campus), the natural path is Foundation → Growth → Ministry Partner, each with a materially better gross margin (82% → 88% → 91%).
3. **Retention mechanics that protect the compounding base**: annual-commitment setup-fee waivers, unlimited-update tiers, and quarterly proactive health-check calls — detailed fully in the business plan's Section 8 — are what keep the mature-phase churn assumption at 3%/month (vs. 4%/month in the unproven early months) rather than drifting higher as the client base grows past what founder-led attention alone can sustain.

## 6. Financial Model Highlights (Year 1 Base Case)

Full detail lives in the linked workbook — headline findings from the `KPI Dashboard` tab:

| Metric | Year 1 Result |
|---|---|
| Active clients, Month 12 | 21 |
| MRR, Month 12 | $3,800 |
| Total Year 1 revenue | $40,973 |
| Year 1 gross margin | 57.9% (blended across the ramp-up and mature-hiring phases) |
| Year 1 EBITDA — full founder draw scenario | **($36,588)** |
| Year 1 EBITDA — before founder draw | **+$5,412** |
| Blended CAC | $1,384 |
| Blended LTV | $5,355 |
| LTV : CAC ratio | 3.9x |
| CAC payback period | 8.6 months |
| Breakeven active client count (at Month-12 fixed cost base) | 46 |

### The single most important finding
On paper, taking a full linear-ramping founder draw ($2,000 → $5,000/mo) alongside a Month-7 contractor hire pushes Year 1 to a **($36,588)** loss against only $10,000 of assumed starting capital — that combination is not fundable from the business's own cash and would require outside savings or income. But the **underlying business, before any owner compensation, is modestly profitable in Year 1 (+$5,412)** and turns EBITDA-positive as early as Month 2 in that lean framing. This is a completely normal and honest picture for a side-business/bootstrapped launch (consistent with running this alongside other ventures) — it means the real Year-1 decision isn't "is this viable," it's "how much personal draw can this specific launch actually support," and the workbook lets that be tuned directly (`Assumptions` rows 28–29) rather than guessed at.

The **breakeven client count of 46** (roughly 2.2x the Month-12 projection of 21) confirms this is a business whose fixed-cost base — mainly the founder's own targeted compensation and the Month-7 contractor hire — is sized for a Year 2 client base, not a Year 1 one. That's the natural argument for either (a) delaying or right-sizing the contractor hire until closer to 30+ active clients, or (b) accepting Year 1 as a deliberately subsidized ramp period, exactly as most single-operator service businesses actually run.

## 7. Year 2 Outlook (Multi-Campus Tier Activated)

A `Year 2 Projection` tab now extends the model through Month 24, picking up exactly where Year 1 ends (21 active clients) and phasing in the **Multi-Campus tier** ($1,000/mo, $4,000 setup, 45 build hours) that Year 1 deliberately left at 0% of mix. Key mechanics:

- **Tier mix ramps, not static targets.** Foundation/Growth/Ministry Partner/Multi-Campus mix shifts linearly from the Year-1 ending split (60/30/10/0) to a Month-24 target of 50/30/15/5 — so Multi-Campus clients show up gradually (first one lands around Month 17) rather than appearing all at once.
- **Acquisition accelerates.** New gross adds ramp from 3/month to 5/month (vs. 1–3/month in Year 1), reflecting a maturing referral/denominational-partnership channel and a contractor who is now fully ramped on builds all year (no founder-only-build exception needed, unlike Year 1's first six months).
- **A second hire (designer/PM) is modeled**, starting mid-Year-2 at $3,000/mo, alongside a founder draw that ramps to $7,000/mo and a contractor retainer that scales to $2,500/mo as build volume grows.

**Modeled Year 2 outcome:** 59 active clients (3 of them Multi-Campus) and **$14,500 MRR** by Month 24, **$170,188 in Year 2 revenue**, and **58.5% gross margin** — up from Year 1's 57.9%, driven by the higher-margin Growth/Partner/Multi-Campus mix. Year 2 EBITDA before founder draw is **+$35,344**, confirming the underlying unit economics scale cleanly once the client base clears the ~46-client breakeven threshold identified in Year 1. Under the full-draw scenario, monthly EBITDA turns positive around Month 23–24 — meaning the two-year combined cash position only turns the corner near the end of Year 2 if the founder takes a full market-rate draw throughout, versus a combined **+$40,756** two-year EBITDA if draw is deferred or minimized until the business is self-sustaining. This reinforces the Year 1 finding: the constraining variable is founder compensation timing, not the viability of the underlying business.

## 8. How to Use the Workbook

The attached Excel file (`Sanctuary_Digital_Business_Model.xlsx`) is fully formula-driven — every blue cell in the `Assumptions` tab is a live input:

- Change pricing, mix %, churn, hosting cost, or the new-client ramp in `Assumptions`, and `Revenue Build`, `P&L`, `Unit Economics`, `Cash Flow & Breakeven`, and `KPI Dashboard` all recalculate automatically.
- The two scenario rows on the `Cash Flow & Breakeven` tab (full founder draw vs. lean/deferred draw) let you stress-test how much personal income this business needs to support in Year 1 without rebuilding any formulas.
- The `Unit Economics` tab isolates per-tier margins so you can test the effect of shifting the client mix (e.g., pushing harder toward Ministry Partner tier) on blended LTV.
